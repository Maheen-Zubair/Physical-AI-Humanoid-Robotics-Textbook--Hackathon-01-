Glossary of Terms

This glossary provides definitions for key terms used throughout the Physical AI & Humanoid Robotics textbook.

A​

Action Generation - The process of converting high-level goals or language instructions into low-level robot control commands, often using machine learning models that map from vision and language inputs to robot actions.

Articulated Robot - A robot with multiple rigid segments connected by joints, allowing for complex movements and manipulations, such as robotic arms or humanoid robots.

Autonomous System - A system that can operate independently without human intervention, making decisions and taking actions based on its programming and environmental inputs.

B​

Behavior Tree - A hierarchical structure used in robotics and AI to organize and execute complex behaviors, where nodes represent conditions, actions, or control flow logic.

Bipedal Locomotion - The act of walking on two legs, a key capability for humanoid robots that requires sophisticated balance control and coordination.

Bounding Box - A rectangular frame used in computer vision to mark the location and size of objects in an image, typically defined by coordinates of the top-left and bottom-right corners.

C​

CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training) - A vision-language model that learns visual concepts from natural language supervision, enabling zero-shot classification and image-text matching.

Cognitive Architecture - A framework for structuring intelligent agents that models human cognitive processes, often used in robotics for planning and decision-making.

Computer Vision - A field of artificial intelligence that enables computers to interpret and understand visual information from the world, typically through image and video analysis.

Control Theory - The mathematical study of dynamic systems and methods for controlling their behavior, fundamental to robotics for ensuring stable and accurate robot motion.

D​

Deep Learning - A subset of machine learning that uses neural networks with multiple layers to model and understand complex patterns in data, widely used in robotics for perception and control.

Domain Randomization - A technique in simulation training where environment parameters (textures, lighting, physics properties) are randomly varied to improve sim-to-real transfer by training robust policies.

Dynamixel - A type of servo motor commonly used in robotics, particularly in humanoid robots, known for precise control and communication capabilities.

E​

Embodied AI - Artificial intelligence that exists within a physical body and interacts with the physical world, as opposed to purely software-based AI systems.

End-Effector - The tool or device at the end of a robotic arm that interacts with the environment, such as a gripper, suction cup, or welding torch.

Episodic Memory - A type of memory system that stores specific experiences or episodes, important for robots that need to remember past interactions and events.

F​

Foundation Model - A large-scale AI model trained on broad data that can be adapted to a wide range of downstream tasks, such as GPT for language or DINO for vision.

Forward Kinematics - The process of calculating the position and orientation of a robot's end-effector based on the known joint angles, used in robot control and planning.

Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM) - A 3D printing technology that creates objects by extruding thermoplastic material layer by layer, commonly used for rapid prototyping in robotics.

G​

Gazebo - An open-source 3D robotics simulator that provides accurate physics simulation, realistic rendering, and convenient interfaces for robotics software development.

General Artificial Intelligence (GAI) - Hypothetical AI that can understand, learn, and apply knowledge across a wide range of tasks at a human level, still largely theoretical.

Gimbal Lock - A phenomenon in 3D rotation systems where two axes align, causing loss of one degree of freedom, often avoided using quaternions in robotics.

GR00T (Generalist Reasoning on Object and Whole-bodies of Things) - NVIDIA's foundation model specifically designed for humanoid robot control using vision, language, and video inputs.

H​

Haptic Feedback - The use of touch and motion sensations to provide feedback to a user, important for teleoperation and human-robot interaction.

Heuristic - A practical approach to problem-solving that uses rules of thumb or educated guesses rather than algorithmic solutions, often used in robot path planning.

Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) - The study of how humans and robots interact and communicate, focusing on making interactions natural and effective.

Humanoid Robot - A robot with a human-like body structure, typically featuring a head, torso, two arms, and two legs, designed to operate in human environments.

I​

Inverse Kinematics - The process of calculating the joint angles required to position a robot's end-effector at a desired location and orientation, crucial for robot motion planning.

Isaac Lab - NVIDIA's framework for robot learning built on Isaac Sim, providing tools for reinforcement learning, imitation learning, and motion planning.

Isaac Sim - NVIDIA's robotics simulation platform built on Omniverse, featuring GPU-accelerated physics and RTX rendering for realistic robot simulation.

Isaac ROS - A collection of GPU-accelerated ROS 2 packages for robotics applications, including perception, navigation, and manipulation.

J​

Joint Space - The configuration space defined by robot joint angles, as opposed to Cartesian space which is defined by position and orientation.

Jacobian Matrix - A mathematical tool in robotics that relates joint velocities to end-effector velocities, used in motion control and optimization.

K​

Kinematics - The study of motion without considering the forces that cause it, fundamental to understanding robot movement and positioning.

Knowledge Graph - A structured representation of knowledge that shows relationships between entities, useful for robots to understand their environment and tasks.

L​

LIDAR (Light Detection and Ranging) - A sensing technology that uses laser light to measure distances and create 3D maps of the environment, commonly used in robotics for navigation.

Locomotion - The ability to move from one place to another, a fundamental capability for mobile robots including walking, rolling, or flying.

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) - A type of recurrent neural network designed to learn from sequences of data, useful for robotics tasks involving temporal dependencies.

M​

Manipulation - The ability to physically interact with objects in the environment, typically using robotic arms and end-effectors to grasp, move, and modify objects.

Marker Detection - The process of identifying and tracking visual markers (like ArUco or AprilTag) in images, used for robot localization and object tracking.

Machine Learning - A field of AI that enables systems to learn and improve from experience without being explicitly programmed, crucial for robot adaptation and skill learning.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) - An advanced control technique that uses a model of the system to predict future behavior and optimize control actions over a finite horizon.

N​

Natural Language Processing (NLP) - A field of AI focused on enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language, essential for voice-controlled robots.

Neural Network - A computing system inspired by biological neural networks, used in robotics for perception, control, and decision-making tasks.

NITROS (NVIDIA Isaac Transport for ROS) - NVIDIA's zero-copy transport system for ROS that enables efficient GPU-to-GPU data sharing between nodes.

O​

Occupancy Grid - A probabilistic representation of space that divides the environment into discrete cells, each containing the probability of being occupied by an obstacle.

OpenVLA - An open-source Vision-Language-Action model developed for robotic manipulation, based on the Llama 2 architecture.

Operational Space - The space in which a robot performs its tasks, typically defined in terms of the end-effector's position and orientation.

P​

Path Planning - The process of determining a sequence of movements for a robot to navigate from a start position to a goal while avoiding obstacles.

Perception - The ability of a robot to interpret sensory information from its environment, including vision, touch, sound, and other modalities.

Physical AI - An approach to robotics that integrates artificial intelligence directly with physical systems, enabling robots to understand and interact with the physical world intelligently.

Point Cloud - A collection of data points in 3D space, typically generated by 3D sensors like LIDAR, representing the external surface of objects or environments.

Pose - The position and orientation of an object in 3D space, typically represented by coordinates (x, y, z) and rotation angles or quaternion.

Q​

Quaternion - A mathematical representation of 3D rotation that avoids gimbal lock, commonly used in robotics for representing orientations and rotations.

R​

Reinforcement Learning (RL) - A type of machine learning where an agent learns to make decisions by receiving rewards or penalties for its actions, widely used in robotics for skill learning.

Robot Operating System (ROS) - A flexible framework for writing robot software, providing services like hardware abstraction, device drivers, and message passing.

ROS 2 - The second generation of the Robot Operating System, featuring improved real-time capabilities, security, and production readiness.

RT-2 (Robotic Transformer 2) - Google's Vision-Language-Action model that treats robot actions as language tokens for end-to-end learning.

Rigid Body - An idealized solid object that does not deform under applied forces, used in physics simulation to model robot links and environmental objects.

S​

Sensor Fusion - The process of combining data from multiple sensors to achieve better accuracy and reliability than could be achieved by using a single sensor.

SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) - The computational problem of constructing or updating a map of an unknown environment while simultaneously keeping track of the robot's location within it.

Social Robot - A robot designed to interact with humans in a socially acceptable way, often featuring human-like appearance or behavior.

Stereoscopic Vision - A method of 3D vision that uses two cameras to capture images from slightly different positions, enabling depth perception similar to human vision.

System Integration - The process of combining various subsystems into a single working system, crucial in robotics for connecting perception, planning, and control modules.

T​

Task Planning - The process of decomposing high-level goals into sequences of actions that a robot can execute, considering constraints and available resources.

TensorRT - NVIDIA's SDK for high-performance inference of deep learning models, used in robotics for accelerating AI model execution on GPUs.

Trajectory Optimization - The process of finding the optimal path for a robot to follow, considering factors like obstacle avoidance, energy efficiency, and task completion time.

Transformer Architecture - A deep learning architecture that uses self-attention mechanisms, fundamental to modern language and vision models including VLA systems.

U​

URDF (Unified Robot Description Format) - An XML format used in ROS to describe robot models, including links, joints, and other properties.

Uncanny Valley - The hypothesis that humanoid objects which appear almost, but not exactly, like real humans cause a feeling of eeriness or revulsion.

Unity - A real-time 3D development platform used in robotics for creating realistic simulations and visualizations.

V​

VLA (Vision-Language-Action) - A type of foundation model that processes visual input, understands language instructions, and generates robotic actions in a unified system.

VSLAM (Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) - SLAM using primarily visual sensors like cameras, often combined with inertial measurement units.

Variable Impedance Control - A control strategy that adjusts the robot's mechanical impedance (resistance to motion) based on task requirements and environmental conditions.

W​

Whisper - OpenAI's automatic speech recognition model that converts speech audio into text, used in robotics for voice command processing.

Whole-Body Control - A control approach that coordinates all degrees of freedom of a robot simultaneously, particularly important for humanoid robots.

Workspace - The space within which a robot can operate, defined by the physical reach and configuration of the robot's manipulator.

Y​

Yaw - The rotation around the vertical (Z) axis of a robot, typically one of the three rotational degrees of freedom along with pitch and roll.

Z​

ZMP (Zero Moment Point) - A concept in bipedal robotics used for balance control, representing the point where the sum of all moments of the active forces equals zero.

Zigbee - A wireless communication protocol commonly used in robotics and IoT for low-power, low-data-rate applications.
